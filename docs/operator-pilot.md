# First operator pilot

The goal is to learn whether one operator can prepare a CSV, understand the
report, and reconcile it with their own records. Running tests or a sample is
not evidence of a completed restaurant pilot.

## Use one period and a small scope

Choose five to ten familiar menu items and one complete reporting period.
Follow the [weekly review guide](weekly-review.md). Keep actual records private;
the ignored `local-data/` folder is available for local work.

Record these facts alongside your inputs:

- Period, currency, and which items or channels are included.
- Whether selling prices are listed prices or averages after discounts.
- Whether ingredient costs are documented recipe costs or owner estimates.
- Source report and recipe/invoice references, kept privately.
- Treatment of returns, comps, modifiers, tax, tips, and delivery fees.

Estimates can help explore a workflow. Do not describe them as recorded sales
or audited costs. Price times quantity is a calculated amount until reconciled
with the corresponding POS sales total.

## Verify and record feedback

1. Run the CSV report and compare units and sales with the same POS scope.
2. Independently calculate one item's ingredient cost and contribution.
3. Record any discrepancy, confusing output, or useful finding.
4. Record the time to prepare and review the data if measuring usability. Make
   a comparable measurement of the previous method before claiming time saved.
5. Create an operator-feedback issue with a sanitized description. Do not post
   private records merely to demonstrate that you completed a pilot.

## Completion criteria

A pilot is complete when an operator has tried the workflow, checked the
numbers, and recorded what was learned, including failures. A successful pilot
does not establish external adoption, profitability improvements, or program
eligibility on its own.
